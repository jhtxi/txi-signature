Putting it in Gmail

  1. Under New emails above, click Copy.
  2. In Gmail, open the gear icon, then See all settings. Stay on the General tab.
  3. Scroll to Signature. Click Create new if you have none. The name is only for you, recipients never see it.
  4. Click into the signature box and press Ctrl V. On a Mac, Cmd V. Under Signature defaults, set For new emails use to it.
  5. On reply/forward use is your call. Signing every reply stacks a block down a long thread. Leaving it off means a forwarded thread carries no way to reach you. Either is fine.
  6. If you do sign replies, make a second signature and paste the Replies and forwards block into it. Two lines, no repeated contact details. Then set On reply/forward use to that one.
  7. Tick Insert signature before quoted text in replies. It sits under your reply instead of below the whole thread, and the stray -- line Gmail adds disappears.
  8. Scroll to the bottom and click Save Changes. Gmail does not autosave this.
  9. Send yourself a test. The brand line should be indigo and the site link should open.

Use Ctrl V, not Ctrl Shift V. Shift pastes plain text and strips every bit of the formatting.

Why it looks plain on purpose

  • Built out of a table. Outlook ignores modern CSS layout. Tables render the same everywhere.
  • Every style inline. Gmail strips stylesheets on receive, so a stylesheet becomes no styling at all.
  • Arial only. No custom font survives an email client. Arial looks identical on Windows, Mac, and phones.
  • No logo image. Image files get blocked in Outlook, show as broken icons, or attach themselves to every email you send. The wordmark is live text.
  • Six lines, plus one each for a booking link and LinkedIn. Nothing else. No banners, no quote of the day, no social icons (LinkedIn is a text link, not an icon), no confidentiality paragraph.
